The King James Bible, 1611, in Akoma Ntoso

The Holy Bible as printed by Robert Barker in London in 1611, in its original spelling, with the Apocrypha. There are 80 books, 1,355 chapters and 36,702 verses here, each book in its own Akoma Ntoso file.

Why this document

James I ordered this translation after the Hampton Court Conference of 1604, and it became one of the most influential books in the English language. It isn’t a law, so it isn’t an intended use of Akoma Ntoso. It does test the standard at scale, with tens of thousands of short, numbered provisions.

How it looks

The stylesheet aims for the feel of the first printing: blackletter type, two ruled columns, a printed head-piece and a large initial at the start of each chapter. It is a suggestion of that page, not a facsimile. The long s is produced by the font, so the XML keeps an ordinary s. The page is the XML itself, styled by CSS alone, with no script and no transformation to HTML.

What the markup shows

  • Structure: Each book is a doc. Chapters and verses are nested hcontainer elements, because Akoma Ntoso has no verse element.
  • Collection: king-james-bible-1611.xml is a documentCollection that holds the title page and points to all 80 books.

Status of the text

  • Source: The text comes from the aruljohn/Bible-kjv-1611 repository. It hasn’t been checked against a scan of the 1611 printing, and errors in that source are carried over.
  • Check: The 66 books of the Old and New Testaments have the same chapter and verse numbering as the modern King James text.
  • Missing: The source has no Rest of Esther, which the 1611 Apocrypha includes. So this copy has 13 of the Apocrypha’s 14 books, with the Epistle of Jeremy and the Song of the Three Children as separate files under later names. Two verses, Ecclesiasticus 1:7 and 17:5, are empty in the source and are left empty here.
  • Not included: The dedication, the translators’ preface, the calendars, the genealogies, the chapter summaries and the marginal notes. Book names are the modern ones from the source, not the 1611 titles. Words the 1611 printing set in roman type, to mark them as supplied by the translators, aren’t marked.

How it was made

The XML was generated by Claude for Grant Vergottini, and validates against the Akoma Ntoso 2.0 schema. It isn’t an authoritative text.
